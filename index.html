How you connect

No one can find you here.

There is no search bar. No suggested friends. No “people you may know.” We didn't forget to build these — we refused to. There are exactly four ways in: two in person, two from afar.

In person

Tap Nearby

Hold your phones close. You each see who you found, and you each decide — approve on the spot, or decide later.

In person

Scan a code

Show your QR code or scan theirs. It expires in two minutes — two people, one moment, both choosing.

From afar

Send an invite

For the people who matter but aren't in the room. Invites are limited, and both sides accept — every time.

From afar

Ask by username

Type an exact @name and they decide. Receiving requests is off by default, so nobody can reach you out of nowhere.

If they're not in your life, they're not in your Enso.

No cold DMs. No profile stalking. No inbox full of people you've never met.

Posting

Post like a person, not a brand.

Three kinds of posts. Three lifespans. You choose how long things live.

24 hours

Stories

The everyday stuff. Here today, gone tomorrow, like it should be.

7 days

Moments

Worth more than a day, but not forever. A Moment slowly fades over the week, then quietly leaves everyone else's view. It stays on your own profile — only you can see it — like a journal nobody else reads.

Forever

Memories

The ones that matter. Memories live on your profile permanently, visible to all your connections. Over the years they become something no other app gives you: a memory lane of your actual life — for your people, and no one else.

No like counts under any of it. Reactions come from people, with names and faces — your sister loved it, your best friend laughed. Not a number. Never a score.

Post text, photos, or video. Everything goes to your connections and no one else.

Messages

Conversations that belong to you.

Every chat on Enso is end-to-end encrypted. Messages are scrambled on your phone and unscrambled on theirs — nobody in between can read them.

Not us. Not ever. We couldn't hand your messages over if we wanted to — all we hold is ciphertext.

How backups work

New phone? Your PIN unlocks your backup. It works together with a key Enso holds, and Enso destroys that key after ten wrong guesses. Your recovery phrase is different: generated on your phone, never sent to us, the one key that is yours alone. Forgot the PIN? The phrase gets you back in. Think it leaked? Regenerate it in one tap; the old one dies instantly, your history stays intact.

Lose both, and your chat history is gone — even we can't bring it back. That's the price of a lock nobody else has a key to, and we think it's worth paying.

Built safe before it was built at all
  • Everyone on the Map is ID-verified. Verification is handled by an independent identity service — we never see or store your document. Only for people 18 and over.
  • Your location is never shared. The only pin on the map is the event's, placed by its organizer. Enso does not know or show where you are.
  • No event happens with fewer than three. The exact address unlocks only after at least three people confirm — no one-on-one meetups with strangers, ever.
  • Fuzzy until you're in. Before you join, you see the neighborhood, not the doorstep.
  • You see who else is going. Name and photo of confirmed attendees — enough to decide, nothing more. Profiles stay locked.
  • Women-only events, built in as an option from day one.

The social app comes first — because meeting new people works better when you have somewhere calm to bring them back to.

Our story
“If I'm probably going to fail, why not fail at the fight that actually matters?”

Why I'm building this.

I kept noticing the same thing everywhere. People sitting together, talking, but their minds still in the phone, scrolling stuff they themselves would call nothing. Everyone has a line ready. “People post anything these days.” “I just use it a little.” “I'm not addicted.” Usually said while scrolling. At meals, before sleep, first thing after waking up. I'm not judging anyone — I've been in some of those chairs myself.

Every time I gave the apps another chance, the same thing happened. I'd install one, open it, and it would drop me straight into the part built to hold my attention. For a while I'd honestly forget that I never wanted to watch any of this. Even when I knew better, my mind would keep me stuck there — short videos, strangers' drama, other people showing off their lives. Some people will say I'm exaggerating. I think most of us have felt exactly this, whether we admit it or not.

Every time I gave the apps another chance, the same thing happened. I'd install one, open it, and it would drop me straight into the part built to hold my attention. For a while I'd honestly forget that I never wanted to watch any of this. Even when I knew better, my mind would keep me stuck there — short videos, strangers' drama, other people showing off their lives. Some people will say I'm exaggerating. I think most of us have felt exactly this, whether we admit it or not.

And it's not entirely our fault. These are some of the biggest companies on earth, and they spend unbelievable amounts to keep things exactly this way — scientists and designers to push screen time up, influencers to keep the stage full, lawyers for when it goes wrong. They make money. A regular user just loses: focus, attention, the ability to sit with a bored moment, and somehow we end up more alone while being more “connected” than ever.

I tried quitting. In a sense I actually did. But quitting had its own cost — I lost touch with my friends' lives, because the only place they shared anything was the one place built to swallow me. And even what they shared wasn't really their life, just the show version of it.

So a thought showed up and refused to leave: can this even be challenged? For months my answer was no. One person, no experience, no budget, no partner, against an industry that eats anyone who tries. Then another thought: most people fail their first attempt anyway. If I'm probably going to fail, why not fail at the fight that actually matters? I had nothing to lose. So here I am.

I'm building the version of social media I think should exist. Calm, private, no score to keep. It will look boring next to an infinite feed — that's on purpose. And I'll be honest: if you feel the current apps do you no harm, keep them. Enso isn't trying to convert anyone. It's for people who already sense something is wrong and just want their people back, without the machine attached.

A place to stay close to the ones who matter — and, when you're ready, a way to meet a few more of them in real life.

— Ashish, founder of EnsoToronto, 2026

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Who can join, and where?

Sign-up is open in Canada, the United States and India — you'll need a phone number from one of those countries. Enso is built for grown-ups staying close to family and friends, so accounts are for people 18 and over.

When is Android coming?

Soon. Enso is in final testing for Google Play, and this page will link to it the day it's live.

How do I report a bug or suggest something?

Shake your phone anywhere in the app and a “Report a Problem” sheet opens right where you are. You can also go to Settings → Support & Policies for feedback, suggestions and live chat — or email support@ensosocial.app.

Can Enso read my messages?

No. Chats are end-to-end encrypted: messages are scrambled on your phone and unscrambled on theirs. All we ever hold is ciphertext.

How do I delete my account?

From inside the app, or by following the steps on our delete account page, which also explains what happens to your data.
